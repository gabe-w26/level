# Legal review pack — Level

Everything a lawyer needs in one place, so the meeting is short and the bill is small.
Nothing here is legal advice; it's a list of what to check and the facts behind each question.

Prepared 24 September 2026. Live site: https://level-wcyc.onrender.com

---

## 1. What Level is

A New Zealand marketplace where homeowners post a job and local trade businesses quote on it.

- A job goes to at most **15** trade businesses in that trade and area.
- A trade has **4 hours** to quote, or their slot passes to the next business.
- A customer receives at most **3** quotes, first in first served.
- Trades pay a **flat monthly subscription** — $30, $50 or $90 + GST by job value band. No per-lead fees, no credits, no tokens.
- Jobs are shared out by fair rotation. **Reviews and spend do not affect who is offered work.**
- If a subscribing trade gets no jobs in a month, they can claim the month back (see §5).
- Currently a **free pilot**: trades pick a plan and pay nothing, and the guarantee is switched off because there is nothing to refund.

Customer contact details (name, phone, email, street address) are withheld from trades until the
customer either shares them with a specific trade or accepts that trade's quote.

## 2. Priority question: NZ patent 594581

**This is the one to answer first, before any money is spent on marketing.**

- **Patent:** NZ 594581, applicant **Myquote Limited**, filed 12 August 2011, expected expiry around 2031.
- **Ownership today:** Myquote Limited is owned by **hipages Group** (ASX: HPG), which also owns
  **Builderscrack**, Level's main competitor. Builderscrack's site footer asserts the patent.
- **What the abstract describes** (paraphrased): a service provider requests contact with a customer's
  job; the customer accepts or rejects; the provider's contact details, or the customer's, are released
  on acceptance.

**What Level does that may be close to it:** a trade quotes on a job, and the customer's contact
details are released only when the customer shares them or accepts the quote.

**Questions for a patent attorney:**
1. Obtain the **granted claims** from IPONZ (not the abstract — the claims are what bind) and confirm the
   patent is still in force, with renewal fees paid.
2. Does Level's flow fall within any granted claim? In particular: does it matter that on Level the
   **trade never requests contact** — the job is pushed to trades automatically by rotation, and the
   customer's acceptance of a quote is what releases details?
3. If there is a risk, what is the cheapest design-around? Options to price up:
   - release contact details **at the moment the job is offered** to the 15 trades (no acceptance step);
   - keep all contact inside Level's own messaging and never release details at all;
   - release details only after a job is marked as hired.
4. What is the realistic exposure for a pre-revenue startup — is this a letter risk or a court risk?
5. Should Level file anything of its own (the reporting-record feature in §6 may be novel)?

## 2b. The name "Level" — clash check

`level.co.nz` and `level.nz` are both registered and **in use by a trading business**: Level, an
"engagement, strategy and design" agency in Whangārei. That's a different industry, but the name is
identical and they were there first.

**Questions:**
1. Search the IPONZ trade mark register for "Level" — is it registered in classes covering software,
   advertising/marketing services or building services (broadly classes 9, 35, 37, 42)?
2. Does a Wellington trades marketplace called Level risk passing off or a Fair Trading Act claim
   against a Northland design agency of the same name? Does it matter that neither is likely to be
   confused for the other?
3. If Level is kept, is a different domain (e.g. `leveltrades.co.nz`) enough distance, or should the
   trading name itself change before any flyers are printed?
4. Worth registering a trade mark of its own? Cost and timeframe?

This is cheap to answer now and expensive to answer after printing flyers and buying ads.

## 3. Documents to review

All live on the site, drafted but never lawyer-checked:

| Document | URL |
|---|---|
| Terms of use | `/terms` |
| Privacy policy | `/privacy` |
| The no-jobs guarantee wording | `/pricing` and `/terms` |

Specific questions:
- Do the terms make clear that Level is **not** a party to the contract between customer and trade?
- Is the limitation of liability enforceable given the **Consumer Guarantees Act** and **Fair Trading Act**?
- Is the privacy policy adequate under the **Privacy Act 2020**, including the outreach use in §4?
- Does anything Level says amount to a **guarantee about the quality of trades**? The site says only what
  has been checked (licence number against the public register, NZBN, an insurance certificate) and the
  date it was checked. Is that wording safe?

## 4. Outreach to trades who aren't customers yet

Level holds a list of trade businesses gathered from their own published websites, and emails them when a
real job matches their trade and area ("here's a free lead").

Built-in safeguards: only business addresses the business published itself; the source page is recorded
for every one; every email identifies the sender and carries a one-click unsubscribe; opt-outs are
permanent; a maximum of 3 unanswered emails; nothing is sent automatically — an admin presses send.

**Questions:**
1. Does this meet the **Unsolicited Electronic Messages Act 2007** as "deemed consent" (a conspicuously
   published business address, and a message relevant to that business role)?
2. Is holding these business contact details, including the occasional named owner, compliant with the
   **Privacy Act 2020**? Is a privacy statement needed on first contact?
3. Same questions for the text-message version, and for a customer recommending a tradie, where Level
   sends **one** invite email on the customer's behalf.

## 5. The no-jobs guarantee

When charging is on: a subscribing trade who receives no jobs in a paid month can claim that month back.
To qualify they must have sent **5 or more quotes** that month (or the number of jobs they were offered,
if fewer than 5 — but the full 5 still applies if they paused during the month).

**Questions:**
1. Is this wording safe under the Fair Trading Act — is "money-back guarantee" the right phrase for it?
2. Must the conditions appear wherever the guarantee is advertised?
3. Any GST treatment issue with refunding a subscription month?

## 6. Progress updates (the new feature)

When quoting, a trade promises progress updates — daily, weekly or monthly. Once hired they post updates,
and Level scores kept-versus-promised and shows that record publicly on their profile
("Keeps customers updated: 94% of promised updates on time").

**Questions:**
1. Publishing a performance score about a named business — any defamation or Fair Trading exposure if the
   trade disputes it? The score counts only what was promised and what was posted, and the thresholds are
   published.
2. Should there be a right of reply, or a way to dispute a score?
3. Does the promise itself become a **contractual term** between customer and trade, and does Level want it to?

## 7. Other things worth a sentence each

- **Building Act:** residential work of $30,000 or more including GST needs a written contract, a
  disclosure statement and a checklist. Level asks the trade to confirm they'll provide these and asks the
  customer to acknowledge it. Is that enough, or does Level take on a duty by mentioning it at all?
- **Restricted building work:** Level shows the licence rule for each trade and publishes only licences it
  has checked. Any risk in showing licence information at all?
- **Reviews:** only customers who hired through Level can review, and reviews can't be bought or removed by
  the trade. Any obligation to publish a takedown or dispute process?
- **Company structure and GST:** the pilot is free, so nothing is being charged yet. What needs to exist
  before the first dollar — company, GST registration, terms accepted at sign-up?
- **Insurance:** does Level need its own public liability or professional indemnity cover before launch?
