On the headline numbers we’re cheaper on the two smaller plans and level pegging on the biggest — and we’d rather say that than pick a dearer competitor to compare against. But the headline isn’t what you end up paying, and here’s why:

  • Their lead price is the start, not the total. On Builderscrack the $79 buys you the right to buy credits, and a job with plenty of tradies chasing it costs more of them. A single worthwhile connection can run past $250. Here, {{ cfg.MAX_QUOTES }} quotes go on a job and you never pay for one.
  • Their job-system price is per user. Fergus and Tradify are priced per person, so the bill goes up every time you put an apprentice on. Docket is flat.
  • The ${{ elsewhere.parts['running the work']['from'] }} one is capped on jobs, not people. That’s why it’s the cheapest, and it’s why it stops being the cheapest once you’re busy.
  • Nothing passes between theirs. You win a job on one and type it into the other. That’s the bit we actually built.

Their prices are what they publish, excluding GST, checked on 26 September 2026. Check them yourself before you decide anything — we’d rather you did.
