On the subscriptions alone we’re cheaper on the two smaller plans and level pegging on the biggest — and we’d rather say that than pick a dearer competitor to compare against. Three things that column doesn’t capture:

  • Theirs isn’t the whole bill and ours is. A won job on a token system costs tokens; a won job here costs nothing. How much is the next section, because it’s not a number anyone publishes.
  • Their job-system price is per user. Fergus and Tradify are priced per person, so the bill goes up every time you put an apprentice on. Docket is one price for the business.
  • The ${{ elsewhere.parts['running the work']['from'] }} one is capped on jobs, not people. That’s why it’s the cheapest in that column, and it’s why it stops being the cheapest once you’re busy.
  • Nothing passes between theirs. You win a job on one and type it into the other. That’s the bit we actually built.

Their prices are what they published, excluding GST. The honest version of the money question: on {{ finding.name }} you can’t work out what a job will cost you before you chase it. To be fair to them, chasing is free — tokens only come off when the homeowner accepts you, and your plan includes an allowance of them. But how many tokens one accepted job costs is set by their algorithm from the trade, the location, the demand and the estimated size of the job, and no dollar-per-token figure is published anywhere. So you can’t tell in advance how many jobs your allowance buys, and if it runs out the overage is charged automatically at a token-pack rate they don’t publish either. On {{ cfg.BRAND }}, {{ cfg.MAX_QUOTES }} quotes go on a job, and winning one costs you nothing.
