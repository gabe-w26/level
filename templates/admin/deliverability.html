the actual test

The checks above can’t tell you what a particular mailbox will do. This sends the genuine article — the same email a tradie would get, about your most recent job — wherever you point it.

  1. Get a score out of ten. Open mail-tester.com, copy the address it gives you, send to it below, then go back and press Check. It names exactly what it docked you for.
  2. Then try a real inbox. Send to a Gmail address you don’t own — a friend’s. Ask whether it landed in Inbox, Promotions or Spam. Gmail treats an address you send to yourself far more kindly than one you don’t, so testing on your own is testing nothing.
  3. And an Outlook or Xtra address if you can. They filter differently, and plenty of tradies are on one.

If it does land in spam

In the order that actually moves the needle:

  1. Send from your own domain, through a proper provider. A personal Gmail is the weakest option there is for outreach — you can’t sign it as your own brand, and Google may suspend the account for bulk sending. Resend, Postmark or SendGrid on leveltrades.co.nz costs nothing at this volume and fixes most of a bad score on its own.
  2. Add SPF, DKIM and DMARC to that domain. Whoever you send through gives you the exact records to paste; the checks above will go green when they’re live.
  3. Warm it up. A brand-new domain sending 240 emails on day one looks precisely like a spammer. Twenty a day for the first week, then build.
  4. Clean the list. Addresses scraped from websites go stale, and bounces hurt your reputation more than almost anything. If more than about 3% bounce, stop and verify the list.
  5. Keep it plain. What we send is plain text, personally addressed, with one link and a working unsubscribe. That is already the right shape — don’t let anyone talk you into HTML templates and tracking pixels.
